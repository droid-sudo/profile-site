My perspective

Helping generously, with roles understood.

I want this decision to begin with empathy and end with clarity—for the intended parents, for me, and especially for the person who may be born.

Why I am open to donating

Family-building can be deeply meaningful—and difficult.

If my background, health information, and willingness to help can give intended parents another responsible path to a family, I am glad to consider it. I’m open to helping LGBTQ+ parents, single parents, and couples of any race or background.

I am not looking for compensation or a transaction. I am looking for a thoughtful human connection, mutual respect, and confidence that the decision is being made with care.

Roles & boundaries

A donor role, not a parenting role

My intention is to be a donor only. I do not seek custody, parental rights, decision-making authority, financial responsibility, or a role in day-to-day parenting. The intended parent or parents would raise the child according to their own family structure and values.

Because assumptions can differ, these expectations should be discussed directly, reviewed by independent attorneys, and written into a known-donor agreement before any sample is released.

The child’s future

Identity should not have to be a mystery.

I support age-appropriate honesty about donor conception. I am open to child-initiated contact once the donor-conceived person reaches adulthood, with the form and boundaries of that contact discussed in advance and reflected in the agreement.

This does not imply a parental role. It means being willing to answer reasonable questions about background, health history, and identity if the person chooses to ask later.

“The best arrangement, to me, is one where generosity is matched by preparation—and where the child’s long-term well-being stays at the center.”
