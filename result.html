Screening & documentation

Transparent with intended parents. Private by default.

Medical and family-health information deserves careful handling. The right records can be shared with serious intended parents and reviewed by the appropriate professionals without publishing sensitive details to the open internet.

A responsible review

Records should inform—not persuade.

I am happy to answer non-medical questions about my history and to help facilitate access to appropriate documentation. I will not interpret test results or present screening as a guarantee.

Who should review the records?

Intended parents should review medical and fertility records with their fertility clinician. Genetic counseling or additional testing may also be appropriate depending on the recipients' history and treatment plan.

When are records shared?

After an initial introduction and a basic mutual-fit conversation, before anyone makes a final decision or incurs significant costs.

Can the clinic require more steps?

Yes. Eligibility, testing, release, quarantine or waiver requirements, shipping, and treatment use are determined by the relevant clinic, physicians, applicable rules, and the signed agreement.

Does screening remove all uncertainty?

No. Screening is useful information, not a promise about fertility, pregnancy, or the health or traits of a future child.

Why the details are not public

Health and family-history records contain sensitive information. Sharing them selectively protects privacy while still giving serious intended parents and their clinicians the information needed for an informed decision.
